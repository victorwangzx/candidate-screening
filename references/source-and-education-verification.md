# Source boundaries and education verification

## Filenames are not candidate evidence

Use a resume filename only to locate the file, display the original attachment name and tentatively identify the candidate; confirm the name against the document body. Ignore all other filename fields in candidate analysis. BOSS recruitment downloads automatically add or alter job titles, cities, salary ranges, years of experience, status labels and similar text. Do not treat these fields as candidate statements, interview outcomes, schedule availability or evidence of a discrepancy. Do not discuss their possible value, compare them with resume dates, or add follow-up questions merely because they differ.

Obtain salary, location, work experience, job intent and interview progress from the resume body, interview records or separately attributed user/recruiter confirmation. The actual JD is a separate source: ignoring a filename does not discard independently confirmed job requirements. An original filename may appear in a source citation or attachment label, but it must not influence scores, fit, confidence or risk assessments. A filename suggesting a next interview does not establish interview status.

## Education timeline: leads, not accusations

When education is relevant to an explicit job requirement, distinguish the earliest disclosed qualification, highest disclosed qualification, degree, major and study mode. A resume listing only a bachelor's degree does not establish the candidate's complete earlier education or admission route.

Check disclosed enrollment/graduation dates, program duration and study/work overlap for missing stages or conflicting accounts. If reliable birth and enrollment dates are already provided, an approximate enrollment age may be used solely as a timeline-checking lead; label year-only calculations approximate and do not invent a birth year. Do not request age solely to screen education, set a universal normal-admission-age cutoff, or use age itself to score ability, integrity, stability or fit.

A later start or short bachelor's program may be consistent with a diploma-to-degree route, but it can also reflect transfer, delayed enrollment, repeat admission, different program structures or inaccurate dates. Age alone cannot establish a prior diploma, study mode, misrepresentation or deliberate concealment. Where the dates and program duration support a concrete question, write: “教育路径待核实；可能存在未列出的前置阶段，不能据此确认专升本或故意隐瞒。” State the actual supporting dates and alternatives without assigning an unsupported probability. If no concrete inconsistency matters to the JD, omit speculative flags.

Ask neutrally: “请补充各阶段院校、入学与毕业年月、学历/学位、学习形式，以及本科入学途径；是否有未列出的专科或其他前置学习经历？” With appropriate authorization, request candidate-supplied credentials or an official verification report. Do not contact schools or third parties without explicit authorization.

Report separately: (1) resume disclosure; (2) timeline question and tentative explanations; (3) candidate clarification; (4) verified result and actual JD impact. Missing information is not proof of deception. Only describe a claim as inconsistent when concrete records conflict; deliberate concealment requires further evidence of intent. Do not silently downgrade a bachelor's claim to a diploma, introduce an unapproved “first qualification” or full-time-only requirement, or reduce scores based solely on age or an unverified education-route hypothesis.
