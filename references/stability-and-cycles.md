# Stability and work-cycle evidence

Assess separately:

1. Employment continuity and future alignment: actual durations, changes, consulting/project status, job-related explanations and expectations.
2. Continued responsibility and outcome verification: how long the candidate owned the work and whether results were followed across enough cycles.

Long tenure does not prove continued ownership. Short tenure does not prove poor performance. Do not infer motives or health/family circumstances.

## Annual budget roles

Map business plan/assumptions → compilation/approval → monthly execution/forecasts → correction → year-end review → next-year changes. Confirm joining dates and personally owned steps.

- About one year may cover one cycle depending on timing, but usually limits sustained-effectiveness evidence. Check completeness rather than assuming either success or failure.
- Two consecutive cycles with continued ownership offer stronger evidence: how did first-year errors and corrections change the next budget?
- Multi-year employment is only a lead; verify actual budget responsibility during those years.
- Short implementations can prove setup, coordination and system delivery, not automatically sustained operating results after launch.

Consecutive cycles are a verification priority, not a universal two-year hiring cutoff. Apply a user's explicitly approved hard gate consistently when one exists.

Record employer/project, dates, relevant ownership period, steps/cycles, followed outcomes, sources and unknowns. Ask about a wrong forecast, correction and next-cycle change. Separate continuity risk from incomplete evidence or future-role misalignment.

Other roles use relevant cadences such as renewals, annual audits, seasonality and product launches; do not generalize budget-specific durations to unrelated jobs.
